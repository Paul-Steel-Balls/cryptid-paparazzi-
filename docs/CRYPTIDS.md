# Cryptid roster (draft)

Grouped by **habitat**. Each group is one map, so a map only ever holds its own group. Within a group, cryptids are tiered by **rarity**, which controls how often they spawn and what their photos pay.

## Rarity rules (proposal)

| Tier | Spawn per night | Photo pay multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Common | 2–3 always on the map | ×1 |
| Uncommon | 1 on the map | ×1.5 |
| Rare | ~30% chance per night | ×2.5 |
| Legendary | ~5%, or summoned by special bait | ×5 |

This puts at most 4–5 cryptids on a map at once, and two nights won't play out the same.

## Details: where the legend comes from, and a gameplay hook for each

### Deep Woods (the current map)
- **Hodag** (Rhinelander, Wisconsin; a hoax by Eugene Shepard in 1893). Slow and stubborn, and charges in a straight line. Easy practice target.
- **Pope Lick Goat-Man** (railroad trestle in Louisville, Kentucky). Stays near bridges and ledges. Lures you toward drops.
- **Dogman** (Michigan; popularised by a 1987 radio song). Fast, and stalks you from behind. You only see him when you turn around.
- **Snallygaster** (Frederick County, Maryland; newspaper accounts from 1909). Swoops down from the treetops, so you have to look up.
- **Bigfoot** (Pacific Northwest; the name took off in 1958; Patterson–Gimlin film, 1967). Already built: hearing, line of sight, bait, traps.
- **Jersey Devil** (Pine Barrens, New Jersey; the Leeds legend; wave of sightings in 1909). Flies between treetops and shrieks. Only lands to eat bait.

### Swamp & Lake
- **Loveland Frogman** (Loveland, Ohio; 1955 report, police sightings in 1972). Hops between banks. Common, and skittish.
- **Lizard Man of Scape Ore Swamp** (Bishopville, South Carolina, 1988). Damages your car/RV if you leave it parked near the water.
- **Skunk Ape** (Florida). Gives off a smell cloud. Follow the stink to find him.
- **Honey Island Swamp Monster** (Louisiana; Harlan Ford's account, 1963). Leaves tracks you can follow.
- **Altamaha-ha** (Altamaha River, Georgia). Surfaces in a fixed river stretch for a few seconds at a time.
- **Champ** (Lake Champlain; Mansi photo, 1977). Surfaces only in calm water at dusk or dawn.
- **Loch Ness Monster** (modern wave from 1933; the 1934 "Surgeon's Photo" was a hoax). Legendary: a single surfacing per night.

### Mountains & Snow
- **Wampus Cat** (Appalachian folklore). Pounces from ledges.
- **Almas** (Mongolia and the Caucasus). Shy, and flees from flash. A long-lens challenge.
- **Yeren** (Shennongjia, Hubei, China). Hides in fog banks.
- **Yeti** (Himalaya; Shipton footprint photo, 1951). Legendary. Only found by following footprints in fresh snow.

### Desert & Badlands
- **Jackalope** (Douglas, Wyoming; 1930s taxidermy joke). Tiny and very fast. Common, low pay, fun to chase.
- **Fresno Nightcrawlers** (CCTV footage from south Fresno, California, 2007). Always in pairs, and only walk in the open.
- **Chupacabra** (Puerto Rico, 1995; a dog-like version later in the US Southwest). Goes for livestock bait.
- **Thunderbird** (from a 1890 *Tombstone Epitaph* story; also a figure in many Indigenous traditions). Soars overhead and casts a huge shadow.
- **Mongolian Death Worm** (Gobi; *olgoi-khorkhoi*). Legendary. Travels underground; you track it by ground tremors.

### Close Encounters (small town at night; UFO-adjacent)
- **Hopkinsville Goblins** (Kelly–Hopkinsville, Kentucky, 1955). Come in groups and peek through windows.
- **Van Meter Visitor** (Iowa, 1903). Drawn to lights and bright signs.
- **Dover Demon** (Dover, Massachusetts, 1977). Only seen for one night, and stays near stone walls.
- **Owlman** (Mawnan, Cornwall, 1976). Sits in church towers and tall trees.
- **Flatwoods Monster** (Braxton County, West Virginia, 1952). Appears where a "meteor" landed. Your camera glitches up close.
- **Mothman** (Point Pleasant, West Virginia, 1966–67). Legendary. Appears near the bridge or the old TNT area. His eyes glow in your flash.

## Open questions
- Should Bigfoot stay **Rare**, or become the Deep Woods **Legendary** in place of the Jersey Devil? He's the most famous name, but he's also the first cryptid players meet.
- Some entries come from living Indigenous traditions (Thunderbird; Wampus Cat in some tellings). They're widely used in pop culture, but treating them as joke monsters is sometimes criticised. Options: keep them with respectful designs, or swap them out. I left the Wendigo out for that reason.
- Map order: Woods → Swamp → Desert → Mountains → Close Encounters (finale)?
